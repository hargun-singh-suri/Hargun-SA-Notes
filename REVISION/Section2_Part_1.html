05Performance

  • How fast the system replies to a request and finishes its work
  • Latency = time for one request to get a response. Throughput = how many requests it handles per second
  • Check the p95/p99 (the slowest 5%/1% of requests) — a good average can still hide a bad worst case

07Scalability

  • The system's ability to keep working well as more users or data are added
  • Vertical scaling = upgrade one machine (more CPU/RAM) — simple, but has a hard ceiling
  • Horizontal scaling = add more machines and split the load across them — needed for real large-scale growth

08Availability

  • The percentage of time the system is actually working and reachable by users
  • Written as "nines": 99.9% ≈ 8.7 hours of downtime/year, 99.99% ≈ 52 minutes/year
  • Each extra "nine" is much harder and costlier to achieve than the last

09Fault Tolerance & High Availability

  • The system keeps serving users correctly even when a server, disk, or network link fails
  • Redundancy = keep spare/backup components ready. Replication = keep multiple live copies of the same data
  • Goal: no single point of failure — one thing breaking should never take the whole system down
11

SLA, SLO, SLI

  • SLA — Service Level Agreement: the legal contract you sign with the customer promising a level of service (e.g. "99.9% uptime, or you get a refund")
  • SLO — Service Level Objective: the internal target your team holds itself to, usually stricter than the SLA (e.g. aiming for 99.95% uptime, to leave a safety margin)
  • SLI — Service Level Indicator: the actual number you measure right now (e.g. "this month we measured 99.92% uptime")
  • Don't turn every number you can measure into a promised objective — pick the few that matter
  • Promise fewer SLOs, but keep them realistic, with room for error
  • Always have a recovery plan ready for when the SLI shows you're missing an SLO
