Reliability vs Availability (often confused)

  • Availability = is the system up and reachable right now?
  • Reliability = does the system keep working correctly, without failing, over time?
  • A system can be available but not reliable — e.g. it responds instantly, but gives the wrong answer
  • Both matter, but they are fixed with different techniques

Graceful Degradation & Circuit Breakers

  • Graceful degradation: if a part fails, show partial or cached results instead of crashing entirely
  • Circuit breaker: temporarily stop calling a failing dependency, so its problem doesn't spread to the rest of the system
  • Both are practical ways to actually achieve the Fault Tolerance goal from Part 1

MTBF & MTTR — the math behind Availability %

  • MTBF — Mean Time Between Failures: on average, how long the system runs before it breaks
  • MTTR — Mean Time To Recovery: on average, how long it takes to fix it once it breaks
  • A higher MTBF or a lower MTTR both push your availability % up
MTBF
avg. time between failures (e.g. 30 days)
Availability = MTBFMTBF + MTTR
MTTR
avg. time to fix a failure (e.g. 1 hour)

Failover Strategies

  • A failover strategy decides what happens the moment your main server or service goes down
Active-Passive
  • One server handles all live traffic; a standby sits idle, ready to take over
  • Simpler to run and reason about
  • Failover takes a little time — the standby has to "wake up"
Active-Active
  • Multiple servers all handle live traffic at the same time
  • If one fails, the others are already serving — failover is near-instant
  • Better use of resources, but harder to keep data in sync across all of them
